Chapter 1

The thesis: everything runs on this device

A PDF often holds what a person would rather not hand to a stranger: a contract, a medical letter, a bank statement. Most PDF tools on the web begin by uploading the file. Recto does the opposite. The site sends the app to your browser once; from then on, rendering, editing, redaction, recognition and signing happen in WebAssembly engines inside the tab, and the result is saved back to your disk.

That is a property of the build, not a promise. The page's Content Security Policy allows connections to this site only, so the browser itself refuses any other destination. The app counts requests to other origins and shows the count, and the script that records this page's clips fails on any request that leaves the origin.

Chapter 2

What you can do

Each plate is a clip of the current build, recorded by a script against the same demo files every time. Press play to watch one. Beside it: what the feature does, where it stops, and the test that holds it.

Mark up

The capsule at the foot of the window turns into the Markup palette: pens, highlighter, eraser and lasso, text boxes, notes, shapes, images, stamps, form fields and signatures. Every mark is a standard PDF annotation and one step of the undo history.

Each annotation type the app writes is read back by both PDFium and pdf.js in CI. Annotation matrix

Sign, and see what was checked

Signs with a .p12 or .pfx certificate as the last step of an export (PAdES-B), then opens the signed file again. On open, each signature reads Intact; Intact, changed later; Changed after signing; Broken; or Cannot check, with one line on what was checked.

It never calls a signature valid or trusted: signer identity, trust and revocation are not checked, and there is no long-term validation. Signature test

Redact, and the text is gone

Redaction removes the text, images and paths under each mark, scrubs matching strings across the file, then reads the exported bytes again. If any redacted text can still be found, the download is blocked.

Images in JBIG2, CCITT or JPEG 2000 cannot be searched and are listed as not searched. The finder suggests matches; it does not decide what is sensitive. Redaction test

The phone edits too

Phones get their own edition, a separate shell on the same engine: a reader first, with one capsule that turns into the editing tools. Pen, highlight, adding text, editing text and signatures work by touch, and a fingertip's wide contact is never taken for a palm.

The other tools stay on desktop and tablet; on a small screen the phone keeps these five, by the owner's decision. ADR-0034

Chapter 3

How it works

The app is a set of static files. Your edits are recorded as operations in a document model, each with its undo step, and nothing is written into a file until you export. Export assembles a new file, opens it in a fresh PDFium worker and checks page count, page sizes and the text under redactions before the download is offered.

PDFium, in WebAssembly, in a worker

The engine that renders pages, reads text and writes annotations is PDFium, the one inside Chromium, compiled to WebAssembly and run in a Web Worker of its own, so a heavy page never blocks a scroll or a click. The interface reaches it by messages. Assembly, repair, recognition and signatures have workers of their own.

ADR-0011 · Architecture

A call gate on the main thread

An audit found that PDFium rendered a page in 18 to 48 ms while the app waited 270 to 470 ms for it: the render queued behind other calls to the one worker, 302 annotation reads among them. A gate between the app and the worker now lets the renders you can see go first and posts the reads behind them. Thumbnails of a 300-page file arrived 29% sooner (2,360 to 1,680 ms), with identical pixels.

Two other ideas were built, measured and not kept. A second PDFium worker bought 16 ms of zoom for 68 MB of memory. The browser's native compression was faster but could change which encoding the Smaller save picks.

Pull request #193 · PF-24, PF-25, PF-26

Glass drawn on the GPU

The capsule and the bars over the page are glass. Where WebGL2 runs on real hardware, one canvas over the reader refracts the page bitmaps as mip-mapped textures, uploaded only when a bitmap changes, and draws only when the scene moves. Wherever text on the glass would fall under 4.5:1 contrast, the glass is pulled towards a solid tone.

On a software renderer, with reduced transparency, more contrast or forced colours, or after any GPU failure, the same pieces fall back to CSS drawn from the same tokens.

GLS-7, GLS-12 to GLS-15

Installable, and offline after one visit

A service worker precaches the app shell and keeps the WebAssembly engines and fonts in caches of their own. The OCR packs, about 22 MB with the engine, download from this site only when you recognise text or choose to keep them. Size gates in CI hold the first paint to 120 KB of script, and a gate is never raised to fit a change.

Chapter 4

Check it yourself

None of these claims asks for trust. Each check takes a minute with your browser's developer tools or the source.

  1. Read the Content Security Policy in the app's HTML. It says connect-src 'self': the browser refuses connections to any other origin.

    apps/web/index.html

  2. Open the privacy button in the app's bar. It reads "On this device" and counts every request to another origin that the browser records. It cannot see WebSocket frames or requests the policy blocked, which never leave the browser.

    external-requests.ts

  3. Open the app once, turn the network off and reload. It still opens a PDF and renders its pages.

    offline.spec.ts

  4. Open the network panel and recognise a scan. The OCR engine and its language pack come from this site, and only when you use them.

    ocr.spec.ts

  5. No telemetry, no account and no cookie. Search the source for document.cookie, sendBeacon or an analytics host.

  6. What stays on your device: the app in the service worker's cache; engines, fonts and kept OCR packs in caches named pdf-editor-*; open documents and their edits in the origin private file system, so a reload can restore them; and closed ones in Recents for 30 days or up to 500 MB. Clear them in the privacy popover, or by clearing the site's data.

    storage.ts · retention.ts

  7. The clips on this page were recorded by a script that logs every request and fails on any other origin. The log is published with them.

    requests.log · privacy.ts

Chapter 5

How it was built

Recto has one owner, a student, who sets the direction and makes the important technical decisions. The code is written by a team of AI agents, organised like a small company and held to a written process: lanes, pull requests, decision records, a quality bar, and CI on three browser engines. All of it is in the repository.

Who decides what

  1. The owner, as the board

    Vision, scope, the name, releases, taste, and anything that speaks for him in public. A question for the board waits for his answer, and his answer wins over any earlier record.

  2. The lead, as chief executive

    A long-running agent session that a scheduled task wakes three times a day. It picks the work from the roadmap, writes short briefs, opens lanes, records each technique decision it settles, merges, and reports to the board in the HQ issue.

  3. Agents hired per lane

    An implementer builds a settled spec inside the files its brief owns and stops to ask on any open question. A reviewer reads risky or multi-file work before its pull request opens. A design critic holds screenshots to the quality bar. Readers search the code and read CI logs, so logs never fill the lead's context. Agents do not hire agents.

Work is routed by role: a judge plans, decides taste and reviews; a builder builds from a settled spec; a reader reads and reports. Effort is chosen per kind of work and measured: every lane is a row in the ledger, every failure a lesson, and A/B experiments decide which role does which work. Routing · Charter · HQ issue

A failure becomes a lesson row the same day, and the standing rules of every brief carry its prevention: no fixed waits in tests, no polling a busy lock.

Lessons

One lane, end to end

  1. Brief

    The owner approves a performance experiment from an audit. The lead writes the brief: the objective, the files in scope, the decisions to cite, the checks.

  2. Build

    A builder agent splits the engine service, adds the call gate, and builds the two ideas that were later dropped, each as its own commit.

  3. Review

    A review in the judge role finds a timer poll, a message sent to a worker about to end, and a compression change that could alter output. One round fixes each, with tests.

  4. CI

    The size gate rejects 1.4 KB more on the first paint, so the early start moves. Then the phone in landscape fails, and the gate is reworked until all 46 checks pass.

  5. Record

    Merged as pull request #193 with three decision rows: PF-24 kept, PF-25 tried and taken out, PF-26 measured and not built.

Chapter 6

What it does not do

  • Office files convert to PDF on desktop and tablet only, behind a flag, after a one-time download of about 70 MB. There is no PDF to Office, and no PDF/A claim.
  • Signatures are never called valid: signer identity, trust and revocation are not checked, and there is no long-term validation.
  • Text is edited one line at a time; paragraphs do not reflow.
  • OCR, Compare and the signing download are tested end to end in Chromium only, for now.
  • No collaboration, no cloud sync and no server API.
  • One document is handled by one single-threaded WebAssembly worker; about 1 GB per document is the design ceiling.
  • It is a public beta and has not been audited by a third party.
